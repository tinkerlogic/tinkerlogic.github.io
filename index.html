Why it matters

Answers are everywhere now. Knowing how to think isn't.

Type any question into a search box or an AI and you get an answer in seconds. That makes one skill more valuable than ever: being able to take an unfamiliar problem apart and reason your way to a solution on your own. It's also the skill kids get the least practice with — so that's the one thing we build, using code as the practice ground.

The approach

A repeatable way to think through any problem.

Your child learns a structured way to approach problems they've never seen before — one they can reuse long after the class ends. Here's what makes it work.

◆

Thinking comes first

◇

Learn by doing

◈

Confidence that compounds

Steady weekly wins, so a hard problem starts to feel like a challenge instead of a wall.

Common questions

Does my child need any coding experience?+

None at all. We start from zero and build up gently. The focus is on thinking clearly, not on prior knowledge.

Why Python?+

Python reads almost like plain English, so beginners spend their energy on the actual thinking instead of fighting confusing syntax. It's the language most schools and universities now teach first.

How big are the groups?+

Small by default — a handful of students — so every child gets attention and time to speak up. One-on-one sessions are available if you'd prefer.

What does my child need to join?+

Just a laptop and an internet connection. Everything else runs in the browser — nothing to install.

What ages is this for?+

Ages 13 and up. The approach works for curious beginners and for teens who've dabbled but want to actually understand what they're doing.
